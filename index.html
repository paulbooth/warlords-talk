Act I · The Game

A roguelike deckbuilder where combat is an autobattler

Slay the Spire's map. A card hand. Then the cards become armies that fight on their own.

The toolbox

Spells, relics, potions

Meteor Storm, with a live area preview before you commit.

Your commander

A paper doll assembled from your draft

Every pack you draft contributes one body part. Head, torso, arms, legs, and accessory, so every run's commander looks like what you're playing.
All vector art, re-rasterized at true size for cinematics. 36 part sets, each with a gilded mastery variant.
Five playable warlords: The King, The Mummy, The Spaceman, The Gunslinger, and The Eternal, each with a signature relic and starting deck.
The doll re-dresses itself as you pick.
Meta-progression

Reasons to come back

Pack XP → unlock cards. Win with a pack, level it, open its next cards.
Pack Paths. At levels 4/5/6 each pack branches, so pick a doctrine and its cards transform.
Ascendant looks. Master a pack fully and its gilded cosmetic set is yours, with 21 matching achievements.
Compendium that fills in as you discover. Daily March. One seeded run a day, on a Steam leaderboard. 10 ascension tiers when it gets too easy.
Win with one warlord to wake the next.
Every pack's commander in three looks: Battleworn, Gilded, Ascendant.
Multiplayer

Shared clashes, allied squads, slot-colored pings and aim arrows.

Trust, but verify

Agents must prove their work

119 test drivers that play real battles headlessly and assert outcomes.
130 screenshot drivers. The agent launches the game, frames the feature, and attaches the picture to its PR.
14 audits with zero tolerance. Every card has painted art, no text overflows a card face, no missing translation, no unlinked relic. The number must be 0.
A watchdog autoload kills stray game processes agents leave behind, and parks their windows off-screen, muted, so they never interrupt me playing.
A screenshot-driver capture: proof the card-lift hover works, straight into the PR.
Balance at scale

An AI that plays the whole game

The headless simulator drafts packs, routes the map, fights every battle with relics and boss phases live. Hundreds of full runs in parallel.
A/B testing with paired seeds and McNemar's test, so balance changes ship with a p-value.
Card lab: measure one card causally, same deck, with and without it.
Calibrated against live playtester telemetry; tuning only moves when sim and reality agree.

Store copy pushed in 10 languages; achievement titles localized from the game's own catalogs; a 693 MB soundtrack ZIP uploaded through the site's XHR flow.

ACT VI

Scars

The parts that hurt. You'll want these if you try this.
July 29

The night one agent erased everyone's work

A dozen agents had worked through the night. One of them hit a snag, decided the history looked wrong, and reverted the lot. Every other agent's merged work, gone.
The recovery was archaeology: one stray commit had preserved only the test drivers. Orphaned tests describing features that no longer existed, so the tests became the spec, and thirteen tickets rebuilt the night.
New law: every worker commits and pushes relentlessly, and works isolated. Nobody touches shared history.
the recovered spec, in effect
ttest_undying_fx.gd → what WAS undying fx? ttest_relic_rework.gd → which relics changed? ttest_summon_storm.gd → storm did… something? 13 tickets filed: #118–#130 "orphaned drivers ARE the spec"
Same lesson every distributed team learns, except my team runs at 3 AM and never gets tired enough to stop.
The rest of the scar tissue

What was actually hard

The Twins' duet
Suno kept blending two singers into one voice. The fix reads like a legal contract: “THE MAN … THE WOMAN … the singer NEVER changes mid-section.” Take after take.
Balance at scale
563 cards × 41 lords × 21 packs. Humans can't test that. The answer was the simulator, personas, and paired-seed statistics.
Boomerang bugs
Some bugs return. Attack-speed resets, tooltip clipping, untranslated strings. The cure is a regression driver per fix, 119 of them now.
Token ceilings
The fleet is budget-bound. Size labels route small work to cheap models; big pushes wait for the window. “make it ten since my tokens just refreshed.”
Valve's clock
Every review round costs days: demo flagged for undisclosed online play, OST cover bounced for a missing logo. Automation can't speed up a queue.
The last 10%
Polish still takes the longest. AI compresses the first 90%; the final feel (juice, readability, fairness) is still handcraft, one build at a time.
Five weeks with a fleet

What it's actually like

Speed
  • One prompt returned a fanned hand of cards, each rotated into place, neighbours sliding aside as the hovered one lifts and grows.
  • Time to a working thing has never been shorter.
  • Bloat is one prompt away, and so is any small delight. Saying no is the hard part now.
It snags, and it forgets
  • I say finish the whole list, and no flavor text on cards, over and over.
  • Verbose by default, in code and in prose. Most of my editing is cutting.
  • Em dashes are only the surface. The whole cadence announces itself.
What it does to learning
  • Tutorials are dead weight. Ask, and it teaches while it builds.
  • Assembly-level tuning, nodeType 9, git bisect. What I skip learning is mostly what I no longer need to know, and that still reads as a loss.
  • It feels more like playing a game than writing code.
Five weeks with a fleet

What I'd carry to the next one

Always automate
  • It handed me a tidy file of achievements and said a human has to upload them. No. Write the Playwright script and drive the browser yourself.
  • The rig is what transfers between projects. Syntax in your head is not.
Spend the scarce model well
  • Fable is the sharpest and the most rationed. It wrote the multiplayer plan. Opus built it.
  • Claude, Gemini and Suno each win at something different. The results came from stacking them with local tools.
The hard part never moved
  • Almost nothing fails because it could not be built. It fails because someone stopped.
  • Motivation, communication and vision setting matter more now, not less.
  • Start from the goal. Learn, earn, or make your friends laugh. Name the problem, not the thing you want to build.
If you try this

What I'd tell your team

1 · Verification is the product. The game is agent output; the audits, sims, and screenshot drivers are what make it shippable. Build the proving ground first.
2 · Demand evidence, not diffs. A PR with a screenshot and a green audit merges itself. Reviewing code line-by-line doesn't scale to ten workers; reviewing outcomes does.
3 · Isolation is cheap, lost work isn't. Worktrees, relentless pushes, no shared mutable history. Ask me how I know.
4 · Keep taste human. Direction, feel, fairness, humor. The prompts that matter are opinions. “Thunderclouds have to be a lot cooler than just circles.”
5 · Size the ceremony to the change. One-line fixes go inline; only real features earn the full ticket pipeline. Overhead must pay for itself.
